Two measures at once

Each neighborhood is colored by where it falls on two measures you choose: blue grows with the horizontal one, magenta with the vertical one, and the dark plum corner is high on both.

  • Start with a preset, such as youth poverty against how many summer jobs program sites are nearby.
  • Or pick any two of 94 measures: young people, need, arrests, shootings and program sites, plus everything from the population explorer.
  • Hover a legend square to see only the neighborhoods in it. Click a neighborhood for all of its numbers.
  • Switch to Tract for finer detail, with much larger margins of error.

Arrests and shootings are counted where they happened, not where young people live. The methodology page explains every measure and its limits.

Methodology · Source · Download CSV of current view
Everything substantive (concept, data to use, desired layout) was handled by the author, a human. The coding and file work were primarily in the hands of Anthropic's Claude and Claude Code, under close direction. The author insisted on quality checks, including: every Census table cell checked against the Bureau's own labels; neighborhood figures checked against City Planning's published 2020–24 neighborhood tables (exact matches on children, residents under 25 and child poverty, and medians within $2); every NYPD and program-site total reconciled with the source files; and a separate recomputation of a sample of neighborhoods from the raw sources. The risks of AI remain, and we would be grateful to hear about any red flags you notice.
